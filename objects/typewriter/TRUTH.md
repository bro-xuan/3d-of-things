## 1. The object

- **Exactly what:** Underwood Standard Typewriter **No. 5**, Underwood Typewriter Co. (New York; factory Hartford). Built late 1900 to c. 1931/32, serials about 100 to 3,885,000 (Polt). Front-strike "visible writer" from Franz X. Wagner's design. We build a **c. 1915–1925 No. 5**: 10-inch carriage, pica type, back spacer and tabulator keys, two-colour ribbon. That is the common form, and it matches the HNF 1915 and Museums Victoria 1924 machines.
- **Early vs later No. 5:** the mechanism stayed the same throughout ("superficially modernized, but retained the same basic mechanism", Polt). Over the run Underwood added the back spacer key (a lever on the left by 1908, a key by c. 1917), the "tabular key" (sources disagree on whether it was there from the start), the see-saw ribbon-colour selector, longer carriage options (12 to 18 in) and new decals c. 1923. Sister models: the **No. 4** has 38 keys / 76 characters (no zero; the period needs shift); the **No. 3** is the wide-carriage machine. The fixed-dog/loose-dog escapement dates from 1900–01 (Dyett, US 673,426).
- **Lettera 22?** It is no better documented. The Underwood has a real repair manual with adjustment figures (TM 10-601), so we stay with the No. 5.
- **Real size:** 390 W × 320 D × 245 H mm (museums give H 240–250), **13.1 kg**.
- **The problem it solves:** prints even, legible letters several times faster than handwriting, and (unlike the earlier understrike machines) the typist can see the line while typing it.
- **Its one big idea (the principle):** every key throws its own type bar through one fixed slotted segment, so all 42 bars hit the **same printing point**. The paper moves, not the type: a spring pulls the carriage all the time, and each stroke lets an escapement release it by exactly one letter space.

## 3. The energy chain

source → transmission → control → output. One line each, naming the parts.

1. **Source (finger):** the finger presses a key about 16 mm. The **key lever** (pivoted at the rear) pulls its **sublever** down (TM 24a).
2. **Transmission (type action):** sublever → **connecting link** → tail of the **type bar**. The type bar pivots on the **fulcrum wire** in the fixed **segment** and swings up through the **type guide**. Its slug hits the **ribbon** (raised by the **ribbon guide**, also called the vibrator) onto the paper on the **platen**. The bar stops against the segment's **abutment ring** at the same moment it meets the platen (TM 56, 60).
3. **Control (escapement):** near the end of the stroke the **type bar's heel** (not the key lever) pushes the **universal bar** rearward. The universal bar pushes the **trip screw** and tilts the **rocker**. On the **down-stroke** the **loose dog** slips off the **escapement-wheel** tooth (trip set for when the type is 1/8 in from the platen), and the tooth drops only about 0.6 mm (0.025 in at the wheel rim) onto the **rigid dog**, so the carriage barely moves while the type is printing. The loose dog springs back ahead of the next tooth. On the **up-stroke** the rocker returns, the rigid dog lets the tooth go, and the wheel turns until the next tooth lands on the loose dog. That completes one letter space (TM 24, 27; Dyett 1901).
4. **Output (power and carriage):** the **mainspring** in its **drum** pulls the carriage **right to left** through the **drawband**. The **rack** under the carriage turns the **pinion**, which drives the escapement wheel through the **pinion pawl**. The same spring powers the ribbon winding, and the ribbon is ratcheted on a little each stroke by the **rocker-arm extension** (TM 15, 28, 34). **Return:** the **carriage-return / line-space lever** first turns the platen 1, 2 or 3 lines (lever → plunger → pawl → **cylinder ratchet**), then pushes the carriage right, rewinding the mainspring. The pinion pawl lets the pinion spin freely, so the escapement wheel does not turn (TM 17–18, 28c).

## 5. The misconception

"People think pressing a key **pushes the carriage along**. In fact a **mainspring** coiled in a drum pulls the carriage to the **left** all the time through a drawband. Each keystroke only rocks the escapement so the wheel can slip **one tooth**, one letter space of 2.54 mm. The escapement lets go in two halves. On the way down the loose dog frees the tooth, which only drops onto the rigid dog, so the carriage barely moves while the type is printing. On the way up the rigid dog frees it, and that is when the carriage actually steps." Designers did it this way so that every step is exactly the same however hard you hit the key, nothing smears while the type touches the paper, and the finger only trips a latch instead of dragging the carriage. Goes in: a callout.

## 7. Mechanism map

What moves and what drives it: time (`speed`, `clock`), a control (name and range), a crank or cam. Which existing motion kinds, experiments and charts it reuses, and anything new (a new mechanism needs a viewer module: references/mechanisms.md).

New kind: **src/viewer/typewriter.ts** (no three.js), chosen when a motion uses `tw_space`, with one film cue, `type` (letters of the word typed; 2.5 is the third letter's strike). Its numbers are **src/viewer/underwood.json**, which the build (objects/typewriter/layout.mjs) reads too; `dipAt`, `swingAt` and the stroke's phases are the same sums in both.

| Control | Set to | Moves (curves sampled by content.mjs from layout.mjs) |
|---|---|---|
| `tw_key_<letter>` (t, y, p, e, w, r, i) | that key's stroke, 0–1 (the strike at ½) | its key lever (16 mm × sin πs at the key), sublever, link (turned about a fixed axis, carried by its lower end) and type bar (123.3° × (sin πs)^1.6: it gathers speed towards the paper) |
| `tw_strike` | the stroke under way, 0–1 | universal bar (lifted by the heel once the slug is 22.2 mm, 7/8 in, from the paper: 1.0 mm at the strike), dog rocker (lift ÷ 7 mm arm: 8.4°, so the dogs move 1.6 mm, more than the 1.3–1.4 mm between them), ribbon guide and the ribbon's middle (9 mm, up before the strike), the ribbon's two runs (swung about their spools) |
| `tw_space` | letter spaces moved: the letter's index + the stroke's step | carriage (−2.54 mm each), star wheel and pinion (1/16 turn), mainspring drum (2.54 mm of band on a 20 mm barrel: −7.3°) |
| `tw_ink_<n>` | 0, or 1 from the n-th strike | the n-th letter, up 0.46 mm from under the paper's face |

The step within a stroke: the loose dog lets go when the slug is ⅛ in (3.2 mm) from the paper (s = 0.456), and the wheel drops 0.025 in at its rim (0.108 of a tooth) onto the rigid dog; the rigid dog lets go once the universal bar is back down (s = 0.619), and the wheel turns the rest. Each is a snap over 4% of the stroke (the spring's). Nothing runs on time: no run button, the film drives it all.

The model's own layout (estimates, layout.mjs `L`): segment radius 45 mm and the printing point 83.7 mm above its centre (√(95² − 45²)), so the bar swings from 5° below level to 118.3°; slots over 170°, one every 4.1°; heel 12 mm behind the pivot; key fulcrum 128 mm behind the centre; sublever 55 mm long, pulled 40 mm from its rod; pinion module 2.54/π (pitch circle 16 × 2.54 mm); star wheel 30 mm across; mainspring barrel 40 mm in a 68 mm cup.

## 9. Simplified or left out

Said on the page too (`credit.extra` or the text).

- **Built in code** (tier C). The segment radius, type-bar length and swing, key dip, star-wheel and pinion tooth counts, rack pitch, ratchet teeth and spring pull are **estimates**. No source dimensions them.
- **Type action:** all 42 keys, sublevers, links and bars are drawn; only the seven keys of the word (t, y, p, e, w, r, i) move. The bars are straight with their slugs set to land square; real bars are bent individually. The bars run round the segment in the keys' order from left to right (on the real machine Q is bar 1 and Y bar 21; here Q is bar 2 and Y bar 22). The links follow their sublever exactly and their heel to within a few mm (a fixed turning axis stands in for a ball joint). The sublevers sit in the key levers' planes.
- **Universal bar:** slides straight up as the heels lift it; the real one is carried on arms.
- **Escapement:** rocker + loose dog + rigid dog + star wheel + pinion + rack, on a front-to-back shaft. The loose dog's spring-back, the backlash pawl, trip and limit screws are left out.
- **Shift:** not modelled (the platen doesn't lift for capitals).
- **Mainspring:** a drum in a cup behind the frame's left corner, a little further back than the real one, so the band's slack is out of sight; the spring inside isn't drawn.
- **Left out:** margin stops and bell, line lock, tabulator, back spacer, ribbon reverse and colour selector, ribbon feed, paper bail and feed rolls, paper release, line-space pawl and detent (the ratchet is drawn), tension knob, space-bar linkage, cover plates and decals.
- **Ribbon:** a strip between two spools; the guide lifts its middle at each stroke, and it doesn't advance.
- **Mixed sources:** the clearances come from the 1955 manual for later Underwood Standards. The 1911 Underwood patent confirms that the No. 5 used the same type-bar-heel → universal bar → fixed/loose-dog wheel arrangement.
